Set the safe up once; the team lives in their own role from then on.

Create the safe. On first launch the manager sets their own name and password. The safe is a single encrypted file on the office computer; it never goes to a server, a cloud or another machine. It waits in the tray after Windows starts, and closing the window doesn't quit it.

Add the records. Each record is one system: title, tax or ID number, user code, password. Import the Excel list you already have — the importer checks the tax-ID checksum, shows the whole queue up front, and never overwrites existing records.

You assign the roles. The manager handles operators; the officer sees and uses passwords; the assistant uses passwords but can't see them anywhere — the copy button isn't even rendered on their screen. When a password's due date passes, a "password due" chip lights up on the record row.

Your daily work. Copy a password with one click; thirty seconds later a neutral line overwrites the clipboard, so nobody finds it afterwards. After five, ten or thirty idle minutes the safe locks itself. Who opened which record, who you added — every line lands in the audit log with your name, and the manager gets a printable KVKK summary in one click.

Optionally wire it to the browser. With the optional Chrome extension, the record you select can be filled into the browser safely: the bridge listens only on your own machine, every fill needs your approval, and each ticket is single-use. Nothing is ever filled without the extension.

Papers scatter, Excel files multiply.
The safe stays locked.

A shared Excel sheet or a WhatsApp thread has no control over passwords: nobody knows who knows them, who used them, or who took them along when they left. In the safe, every line carries a signature.
